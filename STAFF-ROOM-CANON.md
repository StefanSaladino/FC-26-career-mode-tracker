## 21 November 2028: PSG post-match dressing-room assessment

**Setting:** staff-only tactical debrief after Napoli 0–2 PSG (Dembélé 23′; Kvaratskhelia 84′), following Arsenal 2–0 Napoli on 31 October.

- Saladino told his assistant that **Meret's Champions League form has been shocking**, and that **Napoli were not getting the match-saving interventions opposition goalkeepers were making**. This was **not said to reporters**, not overheard by journalists, and was **not a public criticism of Meret**.
- The assistant manager recommended opening up competition for the number-one shirt and trialling young goalkeeper **Peacock against Monza on 26 November**. Saladino agreed it might be time to try him. This advice and the discussion of a possible Udinese/Dortmund continuation were **private plans**, not public club policy.
- The staff did not determine whether PSG's two goals were individually preventable by Meret. Never treat that as fact. Meret's prior contributions remain part of the complete team evaluation.
- The coaching-room goal was to assess Peacock's handling, positioning, one-on-ones, command of the box and composure, rather than declare Meret permanently demoted.

**Confidential narrative consequence:** the Monza opportunity was a meaningful moment in the ongoing relationship between the manager and assistant manager. The assistant's recommendation was tested on the pitch and vindicated in its first outing. This emotional payoff belongs in private ongoing dialogue, not a leak to the Napoli newspaper.

## Permanent story-universe separation rules

1. **Internal conversation remains canon.** Everything discussed between manager and AM can matter to motivation, tactical choices, trust and future selection, even when unseen by the public.
2. **The press are at matches.** They see goals, assists, tactical shape, published team sheets, actual performances, league standings and confirmed football awards firsthand. They are not receiving our user-chat event descriptions as private tips or reports.
3. **Never attribute private statements to Saladino** as press quotes, public criticism or anonymous leaks. The manager only makes public statements if he directly gives one in a press interview, press conference or explicitly instructs the assistant to publish it.
4. **Fans have only public information.** They can argue about results, the observed lineup, form and selections. They cannot respond to staff-only conversations as though they overheard them.
5. **No fourth-wall language** in articles or comments: never say "manager supplied", "manager verified", "user reported", "we weren't given the information", "our archive can't confirm", "the assistant recommended", "in this save", or similar simulation/provenance wording. Write as a football newspaper with match observers.
6. **Uncertainty is still handled honestly internally.** Do not fabricate unsupported shots, saves, match ratings, injuries, quotes or transfers. In press prose simply omit unknown micro-details instead of describing missing user inputs.
7. **Public editorial autonomy** can include commentary on visible defeats or performances, but never invent that the manager privately or publicly criticised Meret. Peacock Team of the Week can be covered on its own merits.
8. **Always track outlet/source scope** for newly recorded statements: `internal-coaching`, `press-conference`, `public-interview`, `on-field`, `official-award`, `public-football-analysis`. Only public scopes can appear in newsroom reports.
