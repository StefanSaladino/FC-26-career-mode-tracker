# Napoli Season Room — Fictional Media / Press Canon

## Purpose
This document is the permanent ledger for fictional journalists, pundits, presenters, outlets and press interactions in the FC26 Napoli save universe.

The media layer is not disposable flavor text. **Once a named media personality or outlet appears, it becomes canon.** Preserve who they are, how they speak, what they have previously said, their relationship with Stefan Saladino / Napoli / Italy, and any receipts that can be brought back later.

This file should be updated whenever a new recurring media personality or outlet is introduced or when an existing one has a meaningful new exchange with Saladino.

---

# CORE PRESS INTERACTION RULE

The assistant manager / AI decides when a fictional press appearance is narratively useful and may initiate it without waiting for the user to request one.

Appropriate moments include, but are not limited to:
- major wins or losses;
- controversial tactical decisions;
- finals / semifinals / title-clinching moments;
- transfer rumors or completed deals;
- player-selection controversies;
- contract disputes;
- Italy national-team windows;
- staff/media conflicts;
- injuries, suspensions or fatigue controversies;
- major player form streaks;
- historical milestones;
- moments where a previous pundit/media take can be revisited.

Do **not** force press after every match. It should feel occasional, organic and meaningful.

## Interaction format
1. The AI chooses an established media personality when one naturally fits, or creates a new one if a different voice/outlet is useful.
2. The AI presents the source clearly: person, outlet and relevant context.
3. The AI asks Stefan Saladino the question **in character as the journalist/pundit**.
4. The AI does **not** invent Saladino's answer in an interactive press appearance.
5. The user answers as Saladino.
6. The substance and tone of that answer become save-universe canon immediately.
7. The journalist may ask a natural follow-up if warranted by the answer.
8. Later articles, pundit segments, fan comments and future press questions may quote, paraphrase or reference the exchange accurately.

The AI may still write fictional quotes for players/staff as editorial color under the normal quote-personality rules, but **manager press answers are user-authored when the scene is interactive**.

# CONTINUITY RULES

Every media personality who appears should retain:
- name;
- outlet;
- role/type (reporter, columnist, TV pundit, radio host, transfer journalist, former player, etc.);
- preferred language;
- tone / personality;
- general football worldview;
- any known club/regional bias;
- relationship with Saladino / Napoli / Italy;
- notable prior questions, claims or controversies;
- whether they have admitted error, doubled down or changed position.

Do not reset a pundit to neutral after a major feud simply because a new article is being written. Likewise, do not make every journalist hostile. The ecosystem should include serious reporters, tactical thinkers, local Napoli voices, national media, rival-club voices, provocateurs and sympathetic interviewers.

Media personas can evolve. A hostile pundit can grudgingly respect results; a friendly reporter can ask a difficult question; a serious journalist can criticize a bad decision without becoming an antagonist.

**Facts always gate the media narrative.** Pundits can have opinions and agendas, but they cannot ignore confirmed match chronology or invent outcomes.

---

# LANGUAGE / CULTURAL VOICE

Italian football media should usually sound Italian.

- Italian journalists/pundits should normally speak/write in natural Italian when the interaction is presented as Italian-language media.
- English may be used for international/UCL media, foreign journalists, or when presenting a translated question for convenience.
- Local Naples radio/press can use stronger local phrasing and selective Neapolitan flavor.
- Do not make all outlets sound like the same generic English football account.
- When useful, provide the original Italian question plus an English translation, but preserve the source-language identity of the persona.

---

# MEDIA ECOSYSTEM TYPES

Use a varied mix over time rather than repeatedly creating the same kind of pundit.

## Post-match reporters
Usually fact-driven. Ask about selection, substitutions, tactical plans, player performances and specific incidents.

## Television pundits
More opinionated. Can create controversy, challenge narratives, take strong positions and later be held to their receipts.

## Newspapers / long-form writers
Best for legacy, tactical, historical and institutional questions.

## Local Naples radio / press
Closer to supporter emotion and city identity. May be more protective of players/staff or more volatile after poor performances.

## National Italian media
More likely to frame Napoli within Serie A, Italy, rival clubs, the national team or broader legacy debates.

## Rival / regional media
Can bring Inter/Milan/Juventus/Roma perspectives without turning every appearance into cartoon hostility.

## Transfer journalists
Ask about bids, contracts, renewals, player happiness, agent stories and market strategy. Rumor ≠ fact.

## Former players/managers
Can carry authority and personal football philosophies. Their prior clubs/history should inform their viewpoint once established.

# PRESS MEMORY / RECEIPTS

Future press appearances should actively use history when relevant.

Examples:
- A pundit who predicted Napoli would lose can be asked about the prediction later.
- A reporter who questioned Pio can revisit the player after another major goal.
- Bellandi can return to the AM subject after a tactical success/failure.
- A transfer journalist who reported Chiesa uncertainty can ask about it again when his status changes.
- A national-team reporter can connect prior Napotalia skepticism to later Italy results.

Do not fabricate an old quote that was never established. If the exact wording is unavailable, reference the prior position without pretending to quote it verbatim.

---

# PRESS SHOULD CREATE STORY, NOT JUST QUESTIONS

The goal is not to simulate repetitive press-conference menus. A good appearance should do at least one of the following:
- reveal Saladino's thinking;
- create or resolve tension;
- deepen a player/staff storyline;
- produce a quote that can matter later;
- establish a pundit's agenda;
- test the manager on a difficult decision;
- generate supporter debate;
- build historical texture around the season.

If the question adds nothing, skip the press appearance.
